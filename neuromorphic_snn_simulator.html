What you're seeing

1. Input: the input layer receives a signal, represented here by a stream of pulses.

2. Integration: each simulated neuron accumulates incoming signal in its membrane-potential value.

3. Spike: when potential reaches the threshold, the neuron fires a brief spike and resets.

4. Propagation: the spike travels to connected neurons, which may then fire in turn.

How to demonstrate it

  1. Start with input strength around 25% and observe sparse firing.
  2. Increase it to 80–100% and watch more spikes propagate.
  3. Raise the firing threshold to make neurons harder to activate.
  4. Click Send one input pulse to trace a single event through the network.
Important: this is an educational software model inspired by spiking neurons. It is not a physical neuromorphic chip, and the simplified firing rules do not reproduce every detail of a biological neuron.
Concepts illustrated: event-driven signaling, membrane potential, firing threshold, spike propagation, and sparse neural activity. Real neuromorphic hardware implements these ideas with specialized circuits and architectures; hardware efficiency depends on the design and workload.
